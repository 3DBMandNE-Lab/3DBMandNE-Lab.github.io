Research

Living human brain tissue as the experimental system

Cortical tissue from neurosurgical resections stays alive in our cultures for weeks and keeps its architecture and cell types. In it we study glioblastoma and the glia that surround it, with single-nucleus RNA-seq, spatial transcriptomics and imaging, and we work on what the findings mean for patients.

Platform

Human brain slice cultures

We established organotypic culture of adult human cortex as a framework for environmental research in neuro-oncology (Life Sci Alliance 2019) and documented the surgical preparation of tissue for research (Acta Neurochir 2023). The platform has been extended with isolation of viable tumour cells for single-cell sequencing (STAR Protocols 2023), injection-free delivery of glioblastoma cells with soft cryogels (Biomater Adv 2026) and the Core2Edge organoid–slice model of infiltration (Nat Protoc 2026). We reviewed the translational uses and the ethical framework of human brain slice work in Life Sci Alliance 2025.

Spatial organisation

How glioblastoma is built

Tumour and host tissue reprogram each other in both directions, shown by spatially resolved multi-omics of glioblastoma (Cancer Cell 2022). Iron-metabolism stress programs in spatially restricted niches prime invasive mesenchymal states (Neuro-Oncology 2026). In IDH-mutant glioma the spatial organisation evolves with grade (Cancer Cell 2026). Spatial transcriptomics with graph-based deep learning improves routine CNS tumour diagnostics (Nat Cancer 2025).

Tumour–host interaction

Glia and immune cells in the microenvironment

Reactive astrocytes build an immunosuppressive environment around glioblastoma (Nat Commun 2019) and release pro-oncogenic chitinase 3-like 1 (Cancers 2019). Myeloid cells releasing IL-10 disable T cells (Nat Commun 2022). Microglia and macrophage recruitment is shaped by ZBTB18 (Commun Biol 2024) and by SMAC mimetics (Cell Death Dis 2024). Sialic acid metabolism wires transcellular connectivity in the tumour (Neuro-Oncology 2023).

In IDH-mutant glioma we are asking whether the same inflammatory transformation happens in the 2-HG-rich, prognostically better IDH1/2-mutant tumours. R-2-hydroxyglutarate does push peritumoral astrocytes towards an inflammatory state, as described in a 2021 preprint.

Models for therapy

FINALE, CAR immune cells and drug response

FINALE builds an autograft, immune-competent human GBM model: patient-matched tumour and cortex, so that unwanted immune responses from mismatched tissue no longer confound what the microenvironment does. mRNA-based multi-functional CAR immune cells, engineered from PBMCs so that T cells, NK cells and monocytes act together, are evaluated in slice cultures and a human GBM model with Tobias Weiss (Zurich); the earlier mRNA CAR-T work is in Clin Cancer Res 2022. A Fondazione AIRC project with Eleonora Vannini (2023–2028) tests the recombinant protein CTX-CNF1 as a way to boost protective immunity against glioblastoma. Pharmacological studies in the platform include meclofenamate decoupling tumour networks (Neuro-Oncology 2021) and mGluR3 inhibition sensitising to alkylating chemotherapy (Cell Death Dis 2021).

CRYON's

Cryopreservation of human brain tissue

Cryobiology can preserve oocytes, ovarian tissue, sperm and arteries. The brain is harder: every freeze–thaw cycle damages tissue, and although whole-brain cryopreservation is offered commercially, what it does to the tissue, including possible long-term epigenetic changes, has barely been studied. CRYON's measures those effects in human brain tissue over several time scales with sequencing and imaging.

Beyond glioma

Meningioma, epilepsy and the community

The same tissue and omics methods serve a multi-omic pharmacogenomic atlas of meningioma that links mutation, methylation and expression to drug response across 179 compounds, and the study of hippocampal gliosis in pharmacoresistant temporal lobe epilepsy (Brain 2023). Dr. Ravi founded and organises the international Neuron-Glia Interface meeting in Freiburg and co-edited the Frontiers in Immunology research topic on spatiotemporal heterogeneity in CNS tumours (editorial).
