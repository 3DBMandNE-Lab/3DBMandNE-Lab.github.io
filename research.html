Research

One tissue system, four questions

Human cortical tissue from neurosurgical resections, kept alive in culture for weeks, is the substrate for nearly everything we do. We ask how glioma reorganises that tissue, how glia respond to injury and implants, what the spatial organisation of a tumour predicts, and how to turn those measurements into tools a surgeon or pathologist can use.

01 · Platform

Human brain slice cultures

We established organotypic culture of adult human cortex as a framework for neuro-oncology (Life Sci Alliance 2019) and documented the surgical preparation of tissue for research (Acta Neurochir 2023). The platform has since been extended: isolation of viable tumour cells from slice cultures for single-cell sequencing (STAR Protocols 2023), injection-free delivery of glioblastoma cells with soft cryogels (Biomater Adv 2026) and the Core2Edge organoid–slice model of infiltration (Nat Protoc 2026).

Working with living human brain tissue raises questions beyond the technical. We reviewed the translational uses and the ethical framework in Life Sci Alliance 2025.

02 · Glioma

Spatial organisation and tumour–host interaction

Glioblastoma is organised in layers that track hypoxia and host response (Cell 2024), and tumour and host tissue reprogram each other in both directions (Cancer Cell 2022). On the host side we have described reactive astrocytes that build an immunosuppressive environment (Nat Commun 2019) and myeloid IL-10 that disables T cells (Nat Commun 2022). On the tumour side, sialic acid metabolism wires transcellular connectivity (Neuro-Oncology 2023) and iron-metabolism stress programs in restricted niches prime invasive mesenchymal states (Neuro-Oncology 2026).

The same methods now apply to IDH-mutant glioma, where spatial organisation evolves with grade (Cancer Cell 2026) and hypoxia-associated niches support transcriptional plasticity (Das et al. 2026, code).

Active: GBM iron metabolism · LGG hypoxia niches · spatial immune networks by multiplex IHC · CAR-T and PBMC in GBM

03 · Glia

Glial response to injury, implants and synaptic signalling

The lab's neuroengineering roots are in the tissue response to chronic neural probes: transcriptional characterisation of the glial reaction to flexible microprobes (Biomaterials 2021), synchrotron tomography of the probe–tissue interface (Sci Rep 2019) and, now, a conserved multi-lineage transcriptional architecture at the electrode interface integrating bulk, single-nucleus and spatial data (Töykkälä et al. 2026, code).

Microglial morphology is read automatically with MicroFace, which maps remodelling zones and bifurcated trajectories during microinjury recovery (iScience 2026). In human slice cultures we study how TNFα acts on synaptopodin and synaptic plasticity, combining human snRNA-seq with mouse slice transcriptomics (Kleidonas et al., under review, code).

04 · Translation

Diagnostics, pharmacogenomics and proteomics

Spatial transcriptomics with graph-based deep learning improves routine CNS tumour diagnostics (Nat Cancer 2025). In meningioma we built a multi-omic pharmacogenomic atlas linking mutation, methylation and expression to drug response across 179 compounds (code). Cerebrospinal fluid proteomics, starting with spontaneous intracranial hypotension (code), extends the same statistical pipeline to a body fluid that can be sampled repeatedly.

Beyond glioma, the group contributes to platelet-rich fibrin and dural-closure studies with the neurosurgical department, and to biomaterial work on collagen crosslinking.

Computation is part of the lab

We write the analysis software we rely on. Atman is a Rust command-line toolkit for proteomics that runs the same commands on Olink, SomaScan, MaxQuant, DIA-NN and Spectronaut matrices and records every input and output by SHA-256 so any result can be traced and rerun. cloupe-to-10x opens Loupe Browser files for Seurat. The Neural ECM resource curates extracellular-matrix components of the brain by cell type. Each paper ships its code as a repository, organised by figure, so a reader can rerun what we ran.
