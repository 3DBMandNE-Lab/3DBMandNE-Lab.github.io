Research

Four themes, one tissue system

Everything below runs on the same substrate: human cortical tissue from neurosurgical resections, kept viable for weeks, measured with single-cell and spatial methods.

3D ex vivo human brain models

We established organotypic culture of adult human cortex as a framework for environmental research in neuro-oncology (Ravi et al., Life Sci Alliance 2019) and have since used it to study glioblastoma infiltration, drug response and the glial response to injury and implants. Recent work extends the model with spatially controlled tumour delivery via cryogel biomaterials and the Core2Edge organoid–slice protocol. We have also written on the translational applications and ethical considerations of human brain slice cultures (Life Sci Alliance 2025).

Spatial multi-omics

Spatially resolved transcriptomics, single-nucleus RNA-seq, proteomics and imaging are integrated to resolve the organisation of brain tumours. This line includes the multi-layered spatial organisation of glioblastoma (Cell 2024), bidirectional tumour–host interdependence from spatial multi-omics (Cancer Cell 2022) and the evolving spatial organisation of IDH-mutant glioma (Cancer Cell 2026). We develop the analysis tooling ourselves; see Software.

Tumour–host interactions

How glioblastoma reprograms astrocytes, microglia and T cells, and how the host tissue shapes tumour cell state. Examples: reactive astrocytes aiding an immunosuppressive environment (Nat Commun 2019), myeloid IL-10 driving T-cell dysfunction (Nat Commun 2022), sialic acid metabolism and transcellular connectivity (Neuro-Oncology 2023), and iron-metabolism stress programs priming invasive mesenchymal states (Neuro-Oncology 2026).

Neuroengineering and clinical diagnostics

The lab's engineering side covers the tissue response to chronic neural implants, automated morphometry of microglia (MicroFace, iScience 2026) and molecular fingerprinting for intraoperative use. Beyond glioma, we apply the same spatial and proteomic methods to meningioma, medulloblastoma, cerebrospinal fluid and spinal cord macrophages.
